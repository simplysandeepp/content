## What's actually being asked
Point-by-point difference between a plain autoencoder and a VAE, then a one-line justification for why VAEs are the generative-capable one.

## Answer

### Architecture
A **standard autoencoder** has an encoder that maps input `x` to a single fixed latent vector `z = f(x)`, and a decoder that reconstructs `x̂` from that exact `z`. Deterministic, point-to-point.

A **VAE** has an encoder that outputs the *parameters of a distribution* over the latent space instead of a single point — `μ(x)` and `σ(x)` defining `q(z|x) = N(μ, σ²)` — then *samples* `z` from that distribution (via the reparameterization trick, `z = μ + σ ⊙ ε`, `ε ~ N(0,I)`) before passing it to the decoder `p(x|z)`.

### Latent representation
Standard AE: an arbitrary, unconstrained point in latent space per input — there's no guarantee nearby points decode to anything meaningful, and the latent space can have gaps/discontinuities. VAE: each input maps to a *region* (a small Gaussian cloud) in latent space, and the KL term pulls all these regions toward a shared standard normal prior `p(z) = N(0,I)` — this keeps the latent space continuous, overlapping, and structured.

### Training objective
Standard AE: minimizes only reconstruction error, e.g. `‖x − x̂‖²` — nothing regularizes the latent space's shape. VAE: maximizes the ELBO, equivalently minimizes the negative ELBO loss
`L(θ,φ;x) = −𝔼_{q_φ(z|x)}[log p_θ(x|z)] + D_KL(q_φ(z|x) ‖ p(z))`
— reconstruction loss **plus** a KL-divergence term regularizing the latent distribution toward the prior.

### Generated output
Standard AE: cannot reliably generate new samples — feeding a random point into the decoder usually gives garbage, because nothing trained the latent space to be sample-able; it's a compression/reconstruction tool, not a generator. VAE: can sample `z ~ N(0,I)` directly from the prior and decode it into a new, plausible `x`, because training explicitly shaped the latent space to match that prior.

### Why VAE is more suitable for generative tasks
A standard autoencoder only ever learns to reconstruct the exact inputs it saw — its latent space has no guaranteed structure, so sampling from it is meaningless. A VAE's KL term is precisely the piece that regularizes the latent space into something smooth and prior-matching, which is what makes "sample a random `z`, decode it" a valid way to generate *new* data rather than just reproduce memorized inputs. ## Exam tip
If asked "why can't you generate from a plain autoencoder," the correct answer is about the *latent space having no imposed structure* — not simply "it's deterministic." Determinism alone isn't the core issue; the missing regularization is.
