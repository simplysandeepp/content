This subject's mid-term uses **Bloom's Taxonomy "K-levels"** printed right next to each question's marks — you'll see them on the real paper as `K1 (3)`, `K2 (4)`, `K3 (5)`, `K4 (8)`, `K5 (10)`. Knowing what each level actually wants is worth more than any other single piece of exam prep here.

| Level | Verb style | Marks seen | What the examiner wants |
|---|---|---|---|
| K1 — Remember | Describe, List, State, Define | 3 | A clean definition/list, straight from memory. No analysis needed — don't overwrite this one. |
| K2 — Understand | Identify, Explain, Define | 4 | Definition **plus** why/how, in your own words — one short paragraph or a labelled list. |
| K3 — Apply | Contrast, Evaluate, Illustrate | 5 | Use the concept on a case: a comparison table, or apply the idea to a concrete example. |
| K4 — Analyze | Identify + formulate, Sketch a plan | 8 | Break a real scenario into parts and build something from it — a problem statement, a plan, a design. |
| K5 — Evaluate/Create | Elaborate, Design a framework | 10 | The biggest answer on the paper: structured, multi-part, synthesising several ideas into one coherent output. |

**Scoring pattern that works across K-levels:** short definition first (1–2 lines) → structured body (numbered points, a table, or named stages — never one big paragraph) → a concrete example or mini-case tied to the question's own scenario if it gives one → for K4/K5, close with a short "why this works" or "limitations" line, since synthesis questions reward judgement, not just recall.
