## The idea, in plain words

**Research methodology** is the systematic science of *how* to do research — the overall
framework and the reasoning behind why a researcher picks one way of investigating a problem over
another. It's easy to confuse this with a **research method**, but they're different layers:

- **Research methodology** = the "why/how we choose" — the logic and strategy behind the whole
  study.
- **Research method** = the "what we actually do" — one specific technique, like running a survey,
  conducting an experiment, or doing a case study.

So methodology is the bigger picture that justifies picking a particular method; the method is one
tool chosen because the methodology says it's the right one for this problem.

## Objectives of research

Every research study is ultimately aiming at one (or more) of these four objectives:

- **Exploratory** — to gain familiarity with a new phenomenon or get fresh insight into it, often
  when little is known yet.
- **Descriptive** — to accurately portray the characteristics of a group, situation, or
  phenomenon as it currently exists.
- **Diagnostic** — to determine how frequently something occurs, or whether it's associated with
  something else.
- **Hypothesis-testing** — to test whether a causal relationship actually exists between two or
  more variables.

## Significance of research methodology

Why does learning this matter at all? Research methodology is significant for:

- **Sound decision-making** — governments and businesses rely on properly conducted research to
  make policy and strategic decisions instead of guessing.
- **Scholars and degree-seekers** — anyone pursuing a higher degree or academic career needs a
  rigorous methodology to produce defensible, credible work.
- **Professionals** — staying current in a field often means being able to read, evaluate, and
  apply new research correctly.
- **Everyday problem-solving** — the same systematic thinking (define the problem, gather
  evidence, evaluate alternatives, decide) is useful well outside formal academic research.

Taken together: methodology is what turns a casual guess into a defensible, repeatable, trustworthy
conclusion. It's the foundation the rest of this unit is built on — every later topic (sources of a
research problem, data collection, analysis, ethics) is really a deeper look at one piece of this
overall framework.

## Exam questions this topic answers

No question in the real mid-term paper or the practice set targets this lecture directly — it's the
foundational orientation topic the rest of the syllabus builds on, not a standalone exam answer. If
a question does ask something like "What is research methodology?" or "Distinguish between research
method and research methodology," the answer is exactly the distinction and the objectives/significance
points above: define methodology, contrast it against method with the example given, then list the
four objectives and the significance points as supporting detail.
