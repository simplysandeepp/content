## What's actually being asked
Compare the two architectures specifically for this use case (editable face attributes), then explain the actual mechanism of latent-space manipulation that makes controlled edits possible.

## Answer

### Traditional GAN: why it struggles here
A traditional GAN generator takes `z ~ p_z` directly, trained against
`min_G max_D V(D,G) = 𝔼_{x∼p_data}[log D(x)] + 𝔼_{z∼p_z}[log(1 − D(G(z)))]`.
`z` must simultaneously encode age, hairstyle, facial structure, pose, and lighting, while being forced into a fixed Gaussian shape that doesn't match how these attributes actually vary. The result is an **entangled** latent space: nudging `z` to make a face look older tends to also shift pose, hairstyle, or background, because there's no single direction that isolates "age" alone. It's also hard to invert a real photo back into a `z` that reconstructs it well, which limits editing real (not just generated) faces.

### StyleGAN: why it's a better fit
StyleGAN maps `z` through `f: Z → W` (8 FC layers) into an intermediate latent `w`, injected at every synthesis resolution via AdaIN:
`AdaIN(x_i, y) = y_{s,i} · (x_i − μ(x_i)) / σ(x_i) + y_{b,i}`.
Because `w` isn't tied to the fixed prior shape, `W` is **more disentangled** — directions in `W` correlate more cleanly with individual attributes. Per-resolution injection also means different detail levels are controlled separately: coarse layers → pose/face shape, middle layers → structural features like hairstyle, fine layers → colour and fine texture (skin detail, wrinkles).

### Latent-space manipulation for controlled generation
The actual mechanism: find a direction vector `Δ_attr` in `W` corresponding to a semantic attribute, then apply
`w' = w + α · Δ_attr`.
`Δ_age`, `Δ_pose`, `Δ_hairstyle` etc. are found either by (a) training a simple classifier/regressor on `w` codes of images labelled with that attribute and taking the direction normal to its decision boundary (supervised direction-finding), or (b) unsupervised decomposition of a large batch of `w` samples to find the dominant axes of variation, then identifying which axis corresponds to which attribute by inspection. Because `W` is comparatively linear and disentangled:
- `w + α·Δ_age` ages/de-ages the face while leaving pose and hairstyle largely intact.
- `w + α·Δ_pose` rotates the head without changing identity or hairstyle.
- Editing only the `w` fed to coarse layers changes facial structure/shape; editing only the `w` fed to fine layers changes skin/colour detail — giving independent control over "image details" versus "facial structure" that a single entangled `z` cannot offer.

### Comparison summary
| Aspect | Traditional GAN | StyleGAN |
|---|---|---|
| Latent used for edits | `Z` (entangled) | `W` (more disentangled) |
| Attribute isolation | Poor — one `z` controls all | Good — per-direction, per-layer |
| Scale-specific control | None | Coarse/middle/fine via style injection |
| Editing real photos | Hard (poor inversion) | Better (inversion into `W`/`W+`) |

## Exam tip
Always tie "disentanglement" back to *why* it happens — `W` isn't constrained to a fixed prior the way `Z` is — rather than stating it as an unexplained fact; that's usually worth separate marks.
