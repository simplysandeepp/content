## What's actually being asked
Pick any four generative model families, explain each briefly, and give one concrete real-world application per model.

## Answer
The four picked below cover genuinely different generative mechanisms — latent-variable, adversarial, iterative-denoising, and autoregressive — rather than variants of the same idea, which is what this kind of question is usually checking for.

### 1. Variational Autoencoder (VAE)
An encoder maps input `x` to a distribution `q(z|x) = N(μ(x), σ(x)²)` over a latent space; a decoder reconstructs `x` from a sampled `z`. Trained by maximizing the ELBO (reconstruction accuracy + a KL term keeping the latent space close to a standard normal prior).
**Application:** drug discovery / molecule generation — a VAE trained on known molecular structures can sample new, chemically plausible candidate molecules from its latent space for virtual screening.

### 2. Generative Adversarial Network (GAN)
Two networks are trained in a minimax game: a generator `G` tries to produce fake samples realistic enough to fool a discriminator `D`, while `D` tries to tell real from fake — `min_G max_D 𝔼[log D(x)] + 𝔼[log(1 − D(G(z)))]`.
**Application:** image super-resolution — upscaling low-resolution photos or satellite images to high-resolution, sharp outputs (e.g., in photo-editing tools and remote sensing).

### 3. Diffusion model (DDPM)
Learns to reverse a fixed forward process that gradually adds Gaussian noise to data; a network `ε_θ(x_t, t)` is trained to predict the noise at each step, and generation runs this reverse process from pure noise back to a clean sample.
**Application:** text-to-image generation — tools like Stable Diffusion generate high-quality, diverse images from a text prompt, now widely used in digital art, marketing creative, and product design mockups.

### 4. Autoregressive Transformer (large language models)
Models the joint distribution of a sequence as a product of conditionals, generating one token at a time: `P(x_1,...,x_n) = Π_t P(x_t | x_1,...,x_{t-1})`, using self-attention (`Attention(Q,K,V) = softmax(QK^T/√d_k)V`) to condition on all previous tokens.
**Application:** conversational AI assistants and code-completion tools (e.g., chatbots, AI pair-programming assistants) — generating coherent text or code one token at a time conditioned on context.

### Why these four, together
VAEs and GANs are both primarily used for continuous data (images) but differ in training stability vs. output sharpness; diffusion models now dominate high-quality image generation precisely because they avoid GANs' adversarial instability; autoregressive transformers dominate discrete sequence data (text, code) where "generate one step at a time" is the natural framing. Seeing all four side by side is usually enough to answer most "compare generative model types" questions on this paper.

## Exam tip
When asked for "any four," pick models from genuinely different mechanisms (not two GAN variants, say) — it signals breadth. Pairing each with a crisp, *specific* real-world use (not just "used in image generation") is usually where marks are lost or won.
