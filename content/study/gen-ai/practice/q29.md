## What's actually being asked
Given the described symptoms, name the exact failure mode, explain why it happens, and prescribe specific fixes with the mechanism each one targets.

## Answer

### Identifying the problem
This is **mode collapse**, not vanishing gradients. The giveaway is the *combination* of symptoms: G is still producing samples confident enough to sometimes fool D, but only from a narrow subset of digit styles — that is a diversity failure, not a "G has stopped learning" failure. If this were vanishing gradients instead, G's output quality would stall or degrade broadly, without the specific signature of "only a few styles, repeated."

Here, D becoming highly confident is a *consequence* of mode collapse, not an independent problem: once G narrows down to a handful of digit styles, D's job becomes easy — recognizing a few repeated patterns as fake is a much simpler classification task than distinguishing the full diversity of real handwriting — so `D(x) → 1` and `D(G(z)) → 0` sharply. D's confidence is a symptom of how easy G made the job, not a separate vanishing-gradient issue.

### Why it happens
- G's objective only needs to fool the *current* D; the cheapest way to do that is often a few modes that already slip past D, rather than covering the whole distribution.
- The standard GAN loss has no explicit term rewarding variety across generated samples — minimizing `−log D(G(z))` per sample says nothing about how different one sample is from another.
- Once G settles on such modes, G's gradient signal comes only from D's current decision boundary, which may not "see" the unexplored regions of style-space at all, so G has little pressure to move away.

### Recommended fixes, with mechanism
1. **Minibatch discrimination** — feed D statistics across the whole minibatch (e.g. pairwise feature distances between samples in the batch), not just one sample at a time, so D can directly flag and penalize batches that look too similar to each other. This gives G a diversity-aware gradient — it can no longer win by submitting near-identical digits within a batch.
2. **Unrolled GANs** — let G's update account for several lookahead steps of how D will respond, instead of reacting to a single frozen D snapshot. This removes the incentive to exploit a blind spot in D that is predicted to close within those unrolled steps.
3. **Switch to a Wasserstein-based loss (WGAN / WGAN-GP)** — the Wasserstein distance is a smoother, everywhere-informative metric between distributions, unlike the JS-divergence-based standard GAN loss, which behaves poorly when `p_g` and `p_data` barely overlap — exactly the regime created by mode collapse. A smoother loss landscape reduces the incentive for G to get stuck exploiting one narrow mode.
4. **Diversity-promoting regularizers** — e.g. a feature-matching loss, or an explicit term rewarding variety across a batch of G's outputs — directly attacks the root cause (no diversity term in the vanilla loss) instead of relying on architectural tricks alone.

Simple shabdo mein: pehle yeh pehchaano ki G ne sirf chhoti si range exploit kar li hai, toh fix bhi usi diversity-gap ko directly target karna chahiye — architecture replace karna zaroori nahi, loss/training-signal mein diversity laana zaroori hai.

## Exam tip
Explicitly write the sentence distinguishing mode collapse from vanishing gradients — this scenario is deliberately constructed so the two can be confused. Examiners are checking whether you understand that "D is confident" can be a *symptom* of mode collapse rather than proof of a separate vanishing-gradient problem.
