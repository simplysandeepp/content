## What's actually being asked
Full VAE architecture and working, a from-scratch ELBO derivation, how reconstruction loss and KL loss each pull on training, and a diagram of the whole pipeline.

## Answer

### Architecture
- **Encoder** (recognition network, params φ): maps input x to the parameters of an approximate posterior, outputting `μ(x)`, `σ(x)`, defining `q(z|x) = N(μ, σ²)`.
- **Latent sampling**: reparameterization `z = μ + σ⊙ε`, `ε~N(0,I)` (see diagram) — keeps the path differentiable while still sampling.
- **Decoder** (generative network, params θ): maps z back to a distribution over x, `p(x|z)`, typically producing `x̂` as the mean of a Gaussian/Bernoulli used for reconstruction.
- **Prior**: `p(z) = N(0,I)` assumed over latent space, so generation at inference just needs a fresh `z ~ N(0,I)` fed through the decoder, no encoder required.

### Working principle
At training time: x → Encoder → `(μ,σ)` → reparameterize → z → Decoder → `x̂` (exactly the diagram above). Loss combines how well `x̂` reconstructs x and how close `q(z|x)` stays to the prior `p(z)`; both networks are updated jointly in one backward pass. At generation time: discard the encoder entirely, sample `z ~ N(0,I)` directly, run only the decoder to get new samples.

### Deriving the ELBO
We want to maximize the marginal likelihood `p(x) = ∫ p(x,z) dz`, but this integral is intractable. Introduce the approximate posterior `q(z|x)` by multiplying and dividing inside the integral:

`log p(x) = log ∫ p(x,z) dz = log ∫ q(z|x)·[p(x,z)/q(z|x)] dz = log 𝔼_{q(z|x)}[p(x,z)/q(z|x)]`

Apply Jensen's inequality (log is concave, so log of an expectation ≥ expectation of the log):

`log 𝔼_{q(z|x)}[p(x,z)/q(z|x)] ≥ 𝔼_{q(z|x)}[log(p(x,z)/q(z|x))]`

Factor the joint `p(x,z) = p(x|z)p(z)` and split the log:

`𝔼_{q(z|x)}[log p(x,z) − log q(z|x)] = 𝔼_{q(z|x)}[log p(x|z) + log p(z) − log q(z|x)]`
`= 𝔼_{q(z|x)}[log p(x|z)] + 𝔼_{q(z|x)}[log p(z) − log q(z|x)]`
`= 𝔼_{q(z|x)}[log p(x|z)] − D_KL(q(z|x) ‖ p(z))`

This gives the Evidence Lower BOund:

`log p(x) ≥ 𝔼_{q(z|x)}[log p(x|z)] − D_KL(q(z|x) ‖ p(z)) = ELBO`

Training maximizes the ELBO (a tractable proxy for the intractable `log p(x)`), equivalently minimizing the negative ELBO:

`L(θ,φ;x) = −𝔼_{q_φ(z|x)}[log p_θ(x|z)] + D_KL(q_φ(z|x) ‖ p(z))`

### The two loss terms
- **Reconstruction loss** `−𝔼_{q(z|x)}[log p(x|z)]`: how well the decoder reconstructs x from the sampled z. In practice approximated with one Monte-Carlo sample of z (thanks to reparameterization), implemented as MSE (Gaussian decoder) or binary cross-entropy (Bernoulli decoder) between x and `x̂`.
- **KL loss** `D_KL(q(z|x) ‖ p(z))`: regularizes the encoder's output distribution to stay close to the prior `N(0,I)`, keeping the latent space smooth and continuous so it's usable for generation. With Gaussian `q(z|x)=N(μ,σ²)` against the standard normal prior, this has a closed form per latent dimension j, summed over all dims:

`D_KL = −½ Σ_j (1 + log σ_j² − μ_j² − σ_j²)`

Total loss = reconstruction loss + KL loss. The two pull in opposite directions — reconstruction wants z to carry as much x-specific information as possible (pushing σ→0), while KL wants `q(z|x)` to collapse toward the prior everywhere. That tension is exactly what keeps the latent space both informative and smooth/generation-friendly.

### Worked example
Illustrative 1-D latent: μ=0.3, σ=0.8. `D_KL = −½(1 + log(0.8²) − 0.3² − 0.8²) = −½(1 + log(0.64) − 0.09 − 0.64) = −½(1 − 0.446 − 0.09 − 0.64) = −½(−0.176) = 0.088`. If reconstruction loss (BCE) for this sample is, say, 45.2, total loss ≈ 45.288 — reconstruction dominates per-sample, but KL matters summed across the whole batch and all latent dims.

## Exam tip
Always show the Jensen's inequality step explicitly — many students jump straight to "ELBO = reconstruction − KL" without deriving it, and that's exactly where marks are lost. Also state clearly that KL is a regularizer, not a measure of reconstruction quality.
