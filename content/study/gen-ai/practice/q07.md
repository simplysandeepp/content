## What's actually being asked
A full explanation of both paradigms, a step-by-step algebraic derivation of Bayes' theorem connecting `P(x,y)`, `P(x|y)`, `P(y)`, `P(y|x)`, and a detailed multi-axis comparison.

## Answer

### The two paradigms
**Generative learning** builds a model of how the data was generated: it estimates the class prior `P(y)` and the class-conditional likelihood `P(x|y)`, which together define the joint `P(x,y)`. Classification/prediction is then a *derived* step via Bayes' theorem, and generation of new `x` falls out for free since the model knows `P(x|y)`.

**Discriminative learning** skips modeling how `x` arises altogether and learns `P(y|x)` (or a direct decision function) straight from data, by minimizing a loss that only cares about getting `y` right given `x`.

### Deriving P(y|x) from P(x|y) and P(y) — step by step

Start from the definition of conditional probability, applied two ways to the same joint `P(x,y)`:

**Step 1.** By definition, the conditional probability of `y` given `x` is:
`P(y|x) = P(x,y) / P(x)`  ... (i)

**Step 2.** By the same definition, applied the other way round, the conditional probability of `x` given `y` is:
`P(x|y) = P(x,y) / P(y)`  ... (ii)

**Step 3.** Rearrange (ii) to isolate the joint probability:
`P(x,y) = P(x|y) · P(y)`  ... (iii)

**Step 4.** Substitute (iii) into (i), replacing `P(x,y)`:
`P(y|x) = [P(x|y) · P(y)] / P(x)`  ... (iv)

**Step 5.** Expand `P(x)` using the law of total probability — since `y` can take any of its possible values `y'`, sum the joint over all of them:
`P(x) = Σ_y' P(x, y') = Σ_y' P(x|y') P(y')`

**Step 6.** Substitute this into (iv) to get the final, fully expanded form of Bayes' theorem:
`P(y|x) = P(x|y) P(y) / Σ_y' P(x|y') P(y')`

This is exactly the canonical form `P(y|x) = P(x|y) P(y) / P(x)`, with `P(x)` written out as a normalizing constant that sums the numerator's shape over every possible class `y'` — it's what makes the posterior probabilities over all classes add up to 1.

### Detailed comparison

| Axis | Generative | Discriminative |
|---|---|---|
| **Mathematical formulation** | Models `P(x,y)` via `P(x\|y)`, `P(y)`; obtains `P(y\|x)` through the derivation above | Models `P(y\|x)` directly as a parametric function of `x` (e.g. `σ(w·x+b)`) |
| **Model training** | Estimate priors and likelihoods from data (often closed-form, e.g. counting/Gaussian fitting) | Iterative optimization (gradient descent) of a conditional loss (cross-entropy, hinge loss) |
| **Decision boundary** | Implicit, indirect — emerges from comparing posteriors across classes; shape constrained by the assumed likelihood form | Explicit, learned directly to separate classes as accurately as possible on the training distribution |
| **Computational complexity** | Can be cheaper to train (closed-form estimates for simple likelihoods like Naïve Bayes) but modeling the full `P(x)` can be expensive for high-dimensional `x` | Training is typically iterative/costlier per step, but avoids ever modeling the expensive high-dimensional `P(x)` |
| **Advantages** | Can generate new data; works reasonably with less labeled data; naturally handles missing features via marginalization | Typically higher classification accuracy with enough labeled data; fewer distributional assumptions to get wrong |
| **Limitations** | Accuracy suffers if the assumed likelihood form (e.g., Gaussian, feature independence) doesn't match reality | Cannot generate new samples; usually needs more labeled data; offers no insight into how the data is structured |
| **Real-world applications** | Image/text generation, data augmentation, anomaly detection, spam filtering via Naïve Bayes | Logistic regression credit scoring, SVM/NN image classifiers, discriminative sequence tagging |

## Exam tip
This is the "show full working" question — examiners specifically want to see the six-step algebraic derivation written out, not just the final Bayes' formula quoted. Losing the intermediate `P(x,y) = P(x|y)P(y)` step and the total-probability expansion of `P(x)` is the single most common way marks get dropped here.
