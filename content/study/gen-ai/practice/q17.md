## What's actually being asked
A clean, from-scratch derivation of the ELBO naming every term — `q(z|x)`, `p(z)`, the reconstruction likelihood, and the KL term — and why maximizing it is the right training objective for a generative model.

## Answer

### Setting up the quantities
- `p(z)` — the **prior**, a fixed, simple distribution over latent space, `N(0, I)`. It's what we assume z looks like before seeing any data.
- `q(z|x)` — the **approximate posterior**, produced by the encoder: `q(z|x) = N(μ(x), σ(x)²)`. It approximates the true posterior `p(z|x)` (how likely each z is, having seen x), which is intractable because it needs `p(x) = ∫p(x,z)dz`.
- `p(x|z)` — the **reconstruction likelihood**, produced by the decoder: how likely x is, given a latent code z.
- `p(x)` — the **marginal likelihood**/evidence we actually want to maximize but can't compute directly.

### The derivation
Start from the log marginal likelihood and introduce `q(z|x)` by multiplying and dividing by it inside the integral:

`log p(x) = log ∫ p(x,z) dz = log ∫ q(z|x) · p(x,z)/q(z|x) dz = log 𝔼_{q(z|x)} [p(x,z)/q(z|x)]`

Because `log` is concave, Jensen's inequality lets us push the log inside the expectation (which only ever decreases the value):

`log 𝔼_{q(z|x)}[p(x,z)/q(z|x)] ≥ 𝔼_{q(z|x)}[log(p(x,z)/q(z|x))]`

Factor the joint `p(x,z) = p(x|z)·p(z)` and split the log:

`𝔼_{q(z|x)}[log p(x,z) − log q(z|x)] = 𝔼_{q(z|x)}[log p(x|z) + log p(z) − log q(z|x)]`

Group the reconstruction term separately from the prior/posterior ratio:

`= 𝔼_{q(z|x)}[log p(x|z)] + 𝔼_{q(z|x)}[log p(z) − log q(z|x)]`
`= 𝔼_{q(z|x)}[log p(x|z)] − 𝔼_{q(z|x)}[log q(z|x) − log p(z)]`
`= 𝔼_{q(z|x)}[log p(x|z)] − D_KL(q(z|x) ‖ p(z))`

That gives the **Evidence Lower BOund**:

`log p(x) ≥ 𝔼_{q(z|x)}[log p(x|z)] − D_KL(q(z|x) ‖ p(z)) = ELBO`

- First term: expected reconstruction likelihood under the approximate posterior — if we sample a z the encoder thinks plausible for this x, how likely is the decoder to reproduce x?
- Second term: `D_KL(q(z|x)‖p(z)) = 𝔼_{q(z|x)}[log q(z|x) − log p(z)]`, the general KL-divergence formula `D_KL(P‖Q)=Σ_x P(x)log(P(x)/Q(x))` applied with `P=q(z|x)`, `Q=p(z)` — penalizes the approximate posterior for straying from the prior.

Negating gives the loss actually minimized in code:
`L(θ,φ;x) = −𝔼_{q_φ(z|x)}[log p_θ(x|z)] + D_KL(q_φ(z|x) ‖ p(z))`

### Why maximizing ELBO supports generative modelling
Since `ELBO ≤ log p(x)` always holds, pushing ELBO up pushes a *lower bound* on the data's log-likelihood up — the model is directly incentivized to assign higher probability to real data under its generative process `p(z) → p(x|z)`. At the same time (shown by the alternate derivation `log p(x) = ELBO + D_KL(q(z|x)‖p(z|x))`), maximizing ELBO narrows the gap between the tractable `q(z|x)` and the true, intractable posterior `p(z|x)` — so the encoder becomes a better and better stand-in for exact Bayesian inference. Together, this is what lets the trained decoder, fed fresh `z~p(z)` at generation time, produce samples that look like they came from the real data distribution — the entire point of a generative model.

### Worked intuition
Illustrative 1-D case: μ=0.0, σ=1.0 (i.e., `q(z|x) = p(z)` exactly) gives `D_KL = −½(1+0−0−1)=0` — zero KL penalty, but then z carries no x-specific information at all, so the reconstruction term `𝔼[log p(x|z)]` would be poor. Conversely μ=2.5, σ=0.05 (very peaked, very x-specific) gives a large `D_KL` but a near-perfect reconstruction. Training the ELBO balances exactly between these two extremes.
