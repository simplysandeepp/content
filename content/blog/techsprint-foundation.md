## What TechSprint Is

TechSprint is the year's flagship student-organized hackathon and event series — a multi-part initiative running from foundational skill-building through to a full competitive hackathon. The idea is to build a progression: foundation → preparation → competition.

This post covers the first piece.

## Foundation Session

Every large event needs planning work that never gets seen. The Foundation session was that — a working session with the core organizing team to lock in the vision, structure, and responsibilities before public-facing work began.

**Format design** — Defined what the full TechSprint arc looked like: what each phase would teach, who it was for, and what "success" meant at each stage.

**Team assignments** — Design, logistics, technical content, marketing, and partnerships. Explicit ownership prevents the diffusion of responsibility that kills student-organized events.

**Community positioning** — TechSprint needed to feel different from a standard hackathon. The intent was to run an event where participants came away having actually learned something, not just competed.

## Why Foundation Work Matters

The events that fall apart in execution usually had unclear ownership from the start. Doing this groundwork — unglamorous as it is — is what makes everything downstream smoother.

The Foundation session is also where the energy is set. If the organizing team is excited and aligned going in, that bleeds through to the events themselves.
